## Abstract:

The “missing women puzzle”, first brought up by Nobel Economics Prize laureate Amartya Sen, refers to the shortfall in the number of women in Asia. However, few researchers have investigated its potential consequences. Our research team focuses on the missing women problem, represented statistically by sex ratios at birth (male at birth divided by female at birth), and one of its biggest consequences: how the missing women of the 1990s and 2000s affect the rural marriage market in China nowadays. Our research is divided into two parts: quantitative and qualitative research. Quantitatively, we found five out of the 31 provinces with the most unbalanced sex ratios and focused on rural areas within these provinces. Through regression analysis and model selection, we found rural males are hurt by unbalanced sex ratios, and the rural marriage market collapses, as shown by an approximately 2% higher male unmarried rate than females as the sex ratio increases. From qualitative aspects, we explored the social impact of the “Missing Women Problem”: how imbalanced sex ratios and broken rural marriage markets lead to antagonism between genders, a reduction in the birth rate, aging populations, and human trafficking. Thus, our research successfully demonstrates the causal relationship between unbalanced sex ratios and the shaky rural marriage market statistically and illustrates how these consequences of missing women endanger our society. We hope our research serves as a warning for people with gender discrimination and brings more attention to “no gender-selective abortion” and feminism from a socio-economic perspective.

## My Main Work:

The research is about the missing woman and the marriage rate. Based on data from the different rural regions and different periods, we are trying to find a relationship between the proportion of birth rate and the marriage rate. We expect that there will be an increasing disproportion on men and women( more men and less women). And for the women, the relatively increasing population of men means more choices and that they are more liable to find a man to get married to. We also expect that there will be a decrease in the marriage rate even under this situation. The reasons for the decrease are probably the high education level stimulating the awareness of the female consciousness and independence, mobility of the population from the rural areas to city areas, and so on.

For my part,I combined the Ising model to simulate how the imbalance between men and women in a specific region will affect the future gender ratio. Ising model is used to demonstrate the emergence of spatial clusters due to local interaction. The presence of external fields is another model extension, which changes the average preferences or actions in the society. According to this model, when the policy remains unchanged, the imbalance of the population proportion will grow increasingly larger, and the imbalance of the population proportion can merely be minimized through the influence of the policy.
